by Frog on Wed Jan 19, 2005 9:32 am

How about roaming tanks, or Desert Rats?

After having mastered Mission number Central Mission 5 in Master Mode against the "Yellow-flamed devil", I tried simply roaming around on the ground in the Fokker Triplane, without taking off, or taking off and then landing and so on, just to see if I could provoke the enemy, including the devil, into committing mistakes. Well, although the Devil is agile, he's not very bright, while the enemy bomber is quite dumb against moving ground targets, as you will find out if you try the following (as to other enemy pilots, the vast majority are not much more efficient than the bomber in this regard).

Simply roam around on the ground, as if your were a tank, and go into tight circles when attacked, occasionally dropping a bomb to get rid of any aggressive pursuer (the bomb drops a few feet behind you - fighting the bomber this way is fun). Don't push the throttle over half-way or you will take off, and don't overdo it with the bombs or you will blow up. Swerve between obstacles or hide (hard CTRL stops plane) behind buildings, and watch them crash...

With practice, you will even get to visit a few tunnels by roaming into them, no bull (tip: you can climb mounds and hills if you stay on the crests) - I have screen shots to prove it, and wish I could share them on the Small Rockets site! It's just as if you were in a tank or a truck, going around the terrain. For example, if you park appropriately behind the derricks and wait for the devil in Central Mission 5, he will aim straight for you and crash into the interceding structures, leaving you free to continue on your roaming, or take off for mission completion under a different format.

Anyway, this opens a window of opportunity, I think, for the wonder programmers of Small Rockets. So how about roaming tanks or other type of vehicles in the upcoming version, with adjustable firing angles? Seems to me this would require only a few tweaks, no? What fun that would be, to chose between a plane, a tank or a machine-gunning Desert Rat!

Keep it coming, Small Rockets!

by Geometrian on Tue Jun 12, 2007 1:43 pm

Yes, I also found that weird; we could fly all the planes, and yes, I do know that some WWI aircraft had rearward facing machine guns on them. In fact, I think one of Manfred Von Richthofen's planes, DR6, had one. [edit: I thought you were thinking WWII because multi-gun planes were more common then.]

With the invention of nitrogen-based explosives towards the end of WWI, WWII planes carried nitrogen-based bombs. This increased the bombing capacity of bombers because nitrogen-based bombs are smaller and more powerful. Bombers suddenly because a lot more important. That, and since bigger planes can carry extra bombs, led to the development of giant bombers in WWII.

When the enemy shot at them, though, they were obviously easy targets. If they flew too high, though, they were sighted by enemy aircraft. Some bombers were lucky enough to get escort squadrons so they could fly out of AA gun range but still be protected. Those that didn't were often given machine gun turrets.

To summarize, this is one reason why WWI aircraft, particularly bombers, had other machine guns. Its advanage to fighter planes is also obvious- that you can shoot at a better pilot if he's behind you. This effective effect, having many machine guns firing in different places is great- provided you have a place to put all that blasted ammo
