Unfortunately I have to agree. Our games were designed in a much gentler, simpler time when all this was fields, children respected their elders and "broadband" was something you used to hold your trousers up. (In other words, late 2000.)

The Internet is completely open and was only ever intended for use by academic researchers working on new and incredibly expensive ways to blow everyone up during the Cold War. Its use as a network linking gamers together was never something envisaged by its creators back in the late 1960s.

The upshot is that there's no easy way to know that the person who is contacting you is who they claim to be. (Even emails are very easy to forge.) For this reason alone, I would not personally recommend allowing minors to access the internet entirely unsupervised and I strongly advise locking your virtual playrooms with a password to keep out undesirables.

Nevertheless, there is something to be said for including some kind of parental contro / word censor in RAS 2, so I'll pass the idea on to Jon.
