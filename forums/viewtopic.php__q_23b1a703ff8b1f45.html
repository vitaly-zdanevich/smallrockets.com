Accuracy

by Geometrian on Thu May 10, 2007 4:10 pm

When I first got the game, I played through it. Then I realized that there was a thing called 'Accuracy'. It was 2%.

Reflecting back I realized that this was probably due to my playing Central Powers Mission 5: The Red Ace. I had tried it on 'Ace' and had finished the mission close to perfectly. I wasn't hit by aa guns much. The reason for this was chiefly that I began shooting at them with machine guns from a very long distance away. In one case, I had left my machine guns blazing for the duration of the mission- just to see what would happen.

Naturally, there was no way to change this terrible score (not including tweaking the player init file) except to fly around and get perfect accuracy to bring it back up. I fired over a half million bullets this way, which raised by accuracy 5%. I gave up on that character and decided to have fun.

When I got my new laptop, I started again. My current accuracy is now 104%. That means that somehow, my bullets hit something more than once. My new theory is that my rockets or bombs hit more than one something at a time.

My whole point in this is a simple suggestion: To make it so that your accuracy is dependent only on the bullets you fired in your last 10 missions or something. That way, it doesn't become exponentially harder to change. If, when you started, you had bad aim, but rose to be an ace with close to flawless accuracy, your current accuracy rating shouldn't be dependent on your time as a newbie.

In all honesty, most of these design quirks are due to the nature of video games and the limitations of the technology.

Imagine walking around all day while looking with one eye through a cardboard tube and the other eye covered entirely. This kind of tunnel vision is how players today currently play _all_ videogames. We don't have Star Trek-style "Holodecks" (yet!), so we have to compromise on simulation accuracy to allow for that.

The tracer bullets are needed because you can only see directly ahead. Remember that cardboard tube? Consider what that does to your peripheral vision. Our normal field of vision is a lot wider than our displays. Without requiring an IMAX cinema as a minimum system requirement, there's really no way around this.

The 'spray' of bullets from the plane is a little exaggerated, but it does reduce the need for ridiculously high accuracy when firing, making the game more accessible for casual gamers. It's also needed because real planes aren't controlled by mice and keyboards!
