There are two sides to this debate.

The simulationists probably don't agree that the radar should be there at all. (RADAR didn't exist during WW1 anyway.)

However, from a purely game design perspective, the radar has a lot of value: it acts as a replacement for your peripheral vision and 'surround' hearing...

The computer screen you're staring at while playing is a glass wall between you and the virtual world of the game. A real WW1 fighter ace would be able to hear the planes behind him, smell the gunpowder, hear the bullets whizzing past him (or tearing holes in the canvas of his aircraft.) He'd also be able to glimpse airplanes whizzing around above and to the sides -- something we can't do with our games because nobody has invented full holographic displays yet.

The radar doesn't tell you exactly where your opponent is: only which direction he's in relative you. He might be above, below or on your level. RAS' designer felt this was a pretty good compromise, but it's still an imperfect solution.

As for going "under the radar", I think I agree with you: it is purely a matter of taste and, given the design issues I mention above, it can never be anything other than a subjective opinion.

It is certainly a more realistic way of portraying the radar feature, but again, WW1 pilots didn't have it back then anyway, so the only "correct" answer would be to have it switched off entirely.
