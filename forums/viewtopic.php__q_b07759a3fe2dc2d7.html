[RAS2D] Diary Entry #3: Another one bites the dust.

by stimarco on Sun Mar 29, 2009 10:21 am

Prototyping a game is done for a very good reason, but this is one I'd hoped not to have to mention: the process can be used to weed out game ideas which simply don't work well, or can't be produced in the budget allotted. (In my case, the budget was, literally, £0.00!)

RAS2D has hit this very problem: the graphical style I'm using is labour-intensive. It took me a whole day just to create one, single, solitary biplane to shoot at. When I started adding up the other graphics I'm going to need, I realised it's going to take me almost a year of solid work -- full time! -- to finish. And that's just not viable for a part-time project like this.

Another problem, and arguably the most important one, is that it's just not proving much fun to play. There's not much tactical thought involved in a 2D dogfighting game. There's no jinking, no ducking and diving, no subtlety. You spend pretty much all the time flying round and round in circles. It is, to put it bluntly, dull. And that's the kiss of death for any game.

Now, I can think of a few ways to fix this flaw, but all of them involve more complex level design and a heavy focus on art and progression. There's nothing to prevent that, but it's already been done and I would need an actual budget (and multiple artists!) to pull it off better than that version. (An additional problem is that the game linked to uses 3D models as the basis for the sprites. I've come to the same conclusion with my own version: it just plain doesn't work with hand-drawn sprites like mine.)

Granted, I'd have preferred to find this out before putting in the work I've done, but better to find out now, than to carry on for months and months only to realise there was such a fundamental flaw when it's far too late to do anything about it.

So... RAS2D has dived into the ground and exploded before it even got a scoring system!

This is, incidentally, how many game developers nail their designs before throwing serious cash at development. Not every great idea can stand on its own. Others -- like RAS2D -- can be made to work, but not necessarily with the budget you have available.

This is normal! I'm off to work on some new ideas. Preferably ones which don't require a team of thousands to implement...
