Hi!

I haven't played 2D shoot'em ups since "Xenon II Megablast" on a DOS machine (nice 320x200 VGA graphics, horrible music) years ago, so when I tried the Star Monkey Demo, I was reminded of the "good old times".

I bought the game almost immediately, but I must say I'm a bit disappointed. Not from a technical point of view, but in terms of game design. Maybe it's just me (At age 32 I could be just too old for fast games), but after playing Star Monkey for some time, there seems to be a fundamental problem that makes it just too frustrating for me.

All 2D shoot'em ups I've played in the past had one thing in common: They were more or less predictable after some time. They were tough (at least for me; Xenon II took me weeks to finish), but they were "fair". Learning the patterns and remembering the locations of power-ups (or credits for buying weapons later) was rewarded. If you had that huge laser, things would be easier for you (at least for the moment; a few screens later that laser would be the absolute minimum). In Star Monkey I feel punished for getting power-ups. If I have larger weapons
- more enemies appear (why?)
- some power orbs simply do not appear

Maybe I'm wrong, but I get the feeling that this is an artificial measure to keep the players from finishing the game too fast. And I suspect it was necessary because there are too many power-orbs and thus too many possible power-ups in too few game levels.
I really wonder what the game would be like with
- less power orbs (if the player misses one or two -- too bad, he/she's in trouble)
- a fixed number of enemies
- different skill levels to choose from (easy, medium, difficult, nightmare).
In my opinition the attempt to keep beginners and and hardcore-gamers happy with one dynamic system has failed. If hardcore-gamers want 500 objects on the screen at once, give it to them. But please let me play the game on an easier skill level.

I only managed to get past the first boss once (I know, that's pathetic...). I've learned the patterns, and I've learned the weak spots of the boss (at least I think so). On the way to the boss I get almost all power-ups, but as I do, it's getting harder and harder to actually get to the boss. Many times I crash just moments before the boss because the screen gets too crowded (but only if I have many power-ups). If I only think about meeting the second boss, knowing that I have to pass the first every time, is not challenging for me, but a major turn-off.

BTW Before writing this text I tried Xenon II on an NT4 machine and I got killed a lot. But I immediately noticed the difference and I each time I get killed I feel that urge to try it again. Xenon II is huge. It takes many levels to get the really big guns. Oh, and I like the weapons shop between the levels were you can buy/sell power-ups.

Ok, I'll stop now. I suspect it's just my personal problem with the game, but I least wanted to tell you, the developers, about it. It's a difficult matter to explain, especially if English is not one's primary language.

Greetings from Bonn,
Germany

Roland

I may not be one of the development team, but I know my 2d shooter - I've played em' all, from Space Invaders to Mars Matrix and beyond.

What you seem to be describing here, some kind of dynamic difficulty setting, is your mind playing tricks on you. It happens to me sometimes - I can storm through to the final boss in a game on my second or third go, and then totally screw up next time and die at the start.

Simply put though, as far as 'shmups' go, Star Monkey is a pushover. My main problem with it is it's sedate pace, but I'll let that pass ;-)

As for the bosses, I haven't noticed them having more than one weak-spot each, and two of the minibosses (the gigatank and the spidery spaceship thingy) don't have ANY weak-points! You just pile tons of ammo into them and they blow up!

Also, comparing star monkey to Xenon 2 is unfair - Star Monkey is based directly on japanese shmup gameplay, and I'd say it's a fair successor in spirit to raiden, and a couple of other similar games. Xenon 2 is by the bitmap brothers, a small, fairly independent UK codeshop, and while Xenon 2 was a good shmup, it was certainly not a patch on the japanese titles doing the rounds in the arcades of the age.

Oh, as for less power orbs appearing - that's bull. They always appear in the same place - usually after destroying a larger enemy, or a COMPLETE enemy fighter wing - if you let one ship survive, you don't get the weaponry, capice? ;-)

As for more enemies appearing, that doesn't happen at all until you beat the game, after surviving the nightmare that is level 3.

And then you start at the beginning again - only with twice the enemies to deal with, and they all seemingly fire more often too!

I got to level 3 again, on the second difficulty setting - I haven't felt so... so.. ALIVE, playing a game for ages!

Okay - I can't think of much more to say, save for that you're seeing things - the game isn't 'cheating' - you just need to further hone your m4d g4m1ng sk1llz =)

In my opinion Star Monkey is very well-balanced. If your ship is highly upgraded then you have a considerably easier time eradicating enemy waves. If you always got powerorbs for destroying certain enemies and waves, that would mean that the better you got the faster you could upgrade your ship. Star Monkey balance this by making the orbs less frequent and harder to obtain the more powerful you are. That way the orbs are still valuable to you.

And the other side of the coin is that if you lose your upgrades, the game will make it easier for you to come back as the orbs will be easier to obtain (for a while), as well as reducing the amount of enemies a little. This unlike some other old classic shoot'em-ups like Xenon or Nemesis where you lost everything and were hard pressed to last for 5 seconds without a single upgrade when you were on the later levels. I think SM gives you an fair chance when you die, unlike the aformentioned games.

As far as learning the attack patterns of the enemies, I can't say I've had any problems. I'm beginning to know them by heart now.

/JiM
