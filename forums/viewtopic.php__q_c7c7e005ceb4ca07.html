Getting Support
(Updated 12-MAY-2009).

* Please DO NOT POST license code and other support requests to the forums! We have a dedicated Support section for this kind of thing. To contact us with specific support issues, please use the Contact Us link at the bottom of each and every Small Rockets web page. (I've just added the same links to the forum pages, so this should be consistent throughout the site.)

* We do not offer replacement licenses to people who bought our games through another publisher (e.g. RealArcade, Yahoo! Games, etc.) We have absolutely no access to their customer databases, license code generators and the like, so we can't help you there. We can help you with in-game support, such as gameplay issues, game crashes and anything else actually within our control. (We did offer to support people who bought games from Fiendish Entertainment, back in the late '90s, but this is only because Small Rockets was set up by most of the same people. This policy has, however, now come to an end. We feel 10 years is plenty long enough!)

* We only guarantee a response within 72 hours. We don't have someone watching over the forums and SR Support in-box 24/7. (In fact, it's just me. I usually do my rounds on Tuesday, Thursday and Sunday evenings, although the exact times and days sometimes vary.)

* Our game demos already contain the full game. There is no "full version" -- your license code acts like a virtual key, unlocking the full game from within the demo. There is nothing more to download.

* Sometimes, our game installer doesn't always manage to register the ".SRK" file type with our games. If you then click on the license code (as described in our Support pages) and nothing happens, or Windows asks which program to use to open the file, this means you need to restart Windows and try again.

* If you have kept the original setup / installer file as a backup, you might want to check if there's a newer version of the demo on the website. Jon sometimes does minor updates to allow for changes to Windows or work around new bugs in graphics card drivers.

* We are aware that the current license code and Small Rockets account system is showing its age. Jon is considering a replacement system, but this is unlikely to appear before the remakes and new games are finally ready for release. (At the time of this update, "Tower of the Ancients" was in the garage, having its innards beaten with the programming equivalent of a 12lb. lumphammer.)

* Your Small Rockets account is completely independent of your Small Rockets forum account. This is a deliberate policy to maintain security.

* Please, please do NOT create a new Small Rockets account if you forget your password. We have a "Forgotten password?" link on the login page precisely for this purpose! (You would not believe how often I find umpteen accounts created by the same user, under the same email address!)



Forum Etiquette.

The Internet has some rules and traditions of its own. Some of these rules apply explicitly to forums like this one, so here are some pointers on how to ask interesting questions.

What is an Interesting Question?

These are questions which aren't trivially answered by doing a few seconds' searching of the existing resources. Many, many newcomers will ask questions that the forum regulars here have answered over and over again. Such "Frequently Asked Questions" -- known as "FAQs" in Internet jargon -- are boring to answer and have the implication that we're here just to do your web-searching and homework for you. So, before you ask your question, make sure it's not a Frequently Asked Question! To do this, use the "Search" box at the top of each forum page. Or check out our Support area, here. You'll often find your question has already been asked. (Note that when you see "FAQ" as a forum or web-page title, it means it's answering one or more Frequently Asked Questions and is not an actual FAQ in itself.)

Why shouldn't you ask FAQs yourself? Because only one person on this forum is actually paid to be here, and he doesn't get paid to be here 24/7. (That'd be me.) The rest of us are volunteering our free time, so please don't nag or beg or demand instant service. Small Rockets isn't a multi-million-dollar multinational corporation with a global network of support personnel. It's currently just one guy -- Jonathan Small -- running it all in his free time. (I'm not Jonathan. I'm Sean. I handle support duties about three evenings a week -- usually Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays, but other commitments means this can vary.)

Therefore, most questions from newcomers will likely be fielded by a volunteer, donating his own free time of his own free will. If you ask a question that challenges him -- that makes him say, "Good question!" -- you'll be far more likely to get a reply. Ask a boring FAQ and most won't even give it a second look.

(Small Rockets used to be a small development company with actual employees, based in the UK. This ended almost five years ago. Jon is now running the whole thing from a spare room in his home in the USA.)


So, where can I find those FAQ posts and documents again?

Our forums can be searched using the "Search" box at the top of each forum page. (Incidentally, the search feature allows you to refine your search if necessary. It's worth doing this if you're searching for a common word or phrase.)

Our Support area contains a bunch of FAQs and walk-throughs, and you'll also find a few additional FAQs in our "Red Ace" forums dedicated to those games.


What if I'm not an SR customer and lost my game license? Can you give me a new license code for it?

We only provide FULL support for people who have bought directly from us. We get a couple of dollars at most from third-party sales, whereas we get to keep (almost) all the money from sales through our own website. For this reason, we demand that any third-party publishers handle their own customer support. Unfortunately, many do not and simply pass their customers on to us. This has reached the point where we are effectively losing money on third-party sales.

The support level we can offer therefore depends on who you bought the game from. We do wish it could be otherwise, but support costs quite a lot of money. Therefore, we will provide basic support for gameplay issues to such customers, but we can only provide replacement licences, etc., to our own customers.

Are there any new games planned?

Yes! Check out our News and Development Diaries forums for more information.

How come there's nobody online for me to play against?

Most of our games are getting on for nine years old. (A couple were updated in 2005.) Games tend to be popular for a short period, but then the gamers move on to new adventures. However, there are still some veterans who frequent our forums. Post a request for a game on there and see what happens!

(Note that you will need GameSpy Arcade for Internet play.)
