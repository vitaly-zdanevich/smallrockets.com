I also left this in the Red Ace Squadron 2 forum.


Good to see all of you checking in here once in a while. I cant get over how avid we are about this game. I go off and play games with more zap and more stuff, but nothing compares to the simplicity of this game and the HONOR that flows through this game community. Posts like Badgers "Final word to RAS" or something like that....show how great this game is...and dont let him fool you....he does just fine.

And all the oldtimers are always available for real-time lessons. I got my instruction from some really great, talented people, two years ago...MO and FZ come to mind, but i also got lesson from many others and from getting my ass kicked and asking questions in chat and private chat.

I encourage newbies to take some of the old timers aside and ask some questions. Knowledge is power and no one is craftier than MO. If he could use boost we would all suck wind all day. All of the aces have their ways of getting it done. I am no ace, but I win lots of games. Here are my own rules.

1. Never run out of boost or rockets. Never. You are deadliest when people think you are weak. Having a rocket in the chamber and some boost available while you are flying around trying to pick up either is a great way to fool someone into think you are relatively harmless. They will attack. Let 'em have it.

2. Anticipate everyone's actions. As MO says, "to catch a fish, you have to think like a fish." For example, if you nicked someone and they kill you, you KNOW they will head for health while you are spawning. When you spawn, get boost if you can, and head for the health pickups. They will be there and just a few bullets will take them out. You dont NEED rockets if you can get on someones butt, healthy or not.

3. Practice alone. Shoot trees and towers and buildings from afar, at the last second, upside down, while boosting, etc. Practice hitting a line of trees while boosting and firing rockets. Do this from different distances. I can get them all from many different heights and distances. It takes practice.

4. Get a buddy that wants to improve as badly as you and practice shooting each other down. One shoots the other evades. Then switch.

5. Try your damndest not to be predictable. Even Aces have patterns that can be used against them. Aces certainly know mine.

6. When you are alone (on the safe side of an island, for example) do NOT fly straight and level. I almost NEVER fly that way. Someone could be sneaking up on you. NEVER give someone an easy shot.

7. Don't leave or enter a pickup in the same direction twice in a row. Predictability is death in RAS.

8. Stay out of the radar as much as you can. Quickly drop low sometimes during dog fights. You will drop off radar and your adversary will have to look for you and you can use those moments to mount a surprise attack.

9. Rockets rise.

10. Most beginners think Aces are uncanny shots because they dont know that Aces fire a two to four rocket-spread when they shoot at a flying target that is any distance away. Close up, as in a dogfight, one rocket is enough.

11. Different planes have different strengths. You need to fly them all to discover their usefullness in different situations. Each player has different strenghts and one plane may be better for some than for others. I fly them all, depending on my adversarys' choice of planes and depending on the map you are flying.

Tender, larger planes (like the Airco) are tougher to fly through tunnels and pickups in the desert. Fast planes (Junkers and Airco) have an advantage in the Islands where using and picking up boost are critical. Slow (Spad and Halberstadt) planes are great in a game with six to eight players and lots of Junkers flying. You do your killing with machine guns rather than rockets in this situation.

Junkers are easily brought down with guns or rockets, have a greater turning radiius than slower planes, stall more easily, have weaker machine guns, but are small, fast, and fairly steady (rather than tender). These are great for zoom and boom taticts (boost with rockets).

Spads and Halberstadts are harder to bring down with rockets or guns, dont stall as easily, have a tight turning radius even at full speed, take a ton of machine gun bullets to down, have very powerfull machine guns. Aircos are highly manueverable, nearly as fast as a Junkers, have better machine guns than Junkers (but nowhere near as good as the Spads or Halberstadts)

11. Use throttle keys (shift (faster) and control (slower) to slow down your plane for better turning performance. MOST of the best pilots are changing speeds a LOT. This takes tons of practice. You need to learn not to stall while doing this or you are dead meat...unless you are USING the stall to fool someone. MOST of the time, this is not a wise tactic against a good player and MOST of the time it is a good tactic against a newbie.

12. Fly by radar. I spend most of my time watching radar and determining which blips represent planes thar are factors in my airspace. Those that aren't I dismiss. Those that are factors, I determine WHERE they are heading and why they are heading that way. Do they need rockets? Are they hurt. Are they coming for me? What does the commentary say? Did they just win a dogfight? If so, they might be wounded and they might be out or low on boost and/or rockets.

The only way I can dogfight effectively is to fly almost entirely by radar. On the contrary and much to his credit and success, XRAY can use the hat switch to use different views to maintain visual contact with the attacker. I am not able to do that. So, I watch the attacker's blip and make sure it does NOT get behind my plane. When I am sure they cant (through the next few seconds) then I use the blip to figure out where to look for them to come into my single front view, I find them and shoot at them.


13. Develop and trust your spider sense. Is a player going in and out of radar view and if so, are they high or low? Why are they doing that. Are they a factor? Can you sneak up on them out of radar, or pretend no interest then boost toward them and rocket them? Can you place a well-timed rocket spread at their pickup destination? What do they usually do after the make a pickup? Do they usually head for the boost station? Do they usually turn left, right?

14. When a player appears to be going through the health canyon on the big island, and I have other fish to fry (like getting some boost or attacking someone else) I will frequently shoot ONE rocket, low to the ground thought the health pickup station. It will pass through and head about halfway down the canyon. It will either explode against the canyon wall or hit the unsuspecting player who is flying below radar. Often this will scare them into crashing if you miss. OR it will make them think they need to fly UP, in which case I will be ready to zoom and boom them while they are looking for the attacker that hit the canyon wall with a rocket. Its just one ploy of many and others have lots of tricks up their sleeves, but it is pretty cool when you are flying away from someone and you hit them with a rocket you fired two or three seconds earlier when you were momentarily flying towards them.

15. When someone is heading towards you there will be a puff of smoke when they fire a rocket. Watch for it. Wait for it. THEN dodge and fire back. You can nail them while they are trying to get visual confirmation that they hit you. That split second is crucial.

16. Weeding your map: Your map does not look like my map. FZ taught me this. If I shoot a tower down on my map, i can fly in the airspace that used to be occupied by that tower. You cant. On your map, you will still see the tower there and you cannot fly through it unless YOU blow up that tower. Watch others shoot at things on your map. They wont go away until YOU destroy them yourself.

When i am waiting for boost or rockets, or when I am waiting for a lone adversary to spawn, I weed constantly with my machine guns, getting rid of obstacles to my plane and my rockets. I remove all the trees towers and buildings that block my view of a pickup. That way I can see someone flying low to the ground (because the trees no longer block my view) but the other player can't SEE ME cuz the trees are still standing in THEIR map.

This can be a tremendous advantage, especially on the big island. Furthermore, by removing buildings and trees, your rockets are free to hit people who are flying low behind them, rather than expend their explosive power on the tree or building you could have removed earlier. You use your machine guns to do this weeding. Dont waste rockets on weeding.

17. You are most vulnerable when you are picking up rockets and boost. Dont get more than you need. I find 20 rockets is always more than enough. I like having more than ten, but i will not risk being downed if I already have six or seven rockets on hand. For most engagements, that is all you could ever need. Why get shot down trying to play it safe by having a lot of rockets. Instead, play it safe by not spending too much time getting rockets. Enough is enough. Get more when things are not dangerous, not when people are on you.


18. As I have stated, I am looking at the radar a LOT. But i also read nearly ALL the running commentary so that I know what is happening to EVERYONE on the map at all times. Good pilots pretty much know who just ate it. Who is wounded. Who is loaded. Who has boost. Who is spawning. Who isn't loaded.


19. Boosting planes show up brightly in radar. If a plane is bright and coming towards you, you better move QUICKLY. Rockets are on the way. This is another reason you cannot take your eyes of the radar for very long.


20. But most of all, you have to PRACTICE. For example, I wasnt satisfied until I could hit (with rockets) all the trees that stand in rows with one pass, while boosting. This is basic target practice. You need to decide on a target (to the left or right of you) while you are flying straight, then instantly turn and fire and hit it with one shot. Speed kills. Try increasing your reaction speed. You must be able to shoot first and dodge during head on attacks else you will just exchange kill points. Yours for theirs. You can win cames splitting points and getting lucky, but its far better to survive. Teach your brain to fire with LESS information. Teach your brain NOT to get visual confirmation on a rocket shot, but instead, to make the next move (for example evade or burst and shoot). You CAN get faster. You have to to win in this game. Train yourself while flying alone. See how fast you can select an out of sight target (in your mind...like a tower that is not in view but you know it is there), aquire it, and fire instantly or boost and fire mulitple rockets as you approach the target. Takes practice but it bears fruit.

Good lluck all.

KPAX

I will post more if you want them.

Another suggestion, follow the Dicta Boelcke.
Dicta Boelcke

1. Always try to secure an advantageous position before attacking. Climb before and during the approach in order to surprise the enemy from above, and dive on him swiftly from the rear when the moment to attack is at hand.
2. Try to place yourself between the sun and the enemy. This puts the glare of the sun in the enemy's eyes and makes it difficult to see you and impossible for him to shoot with any accuracy.
3. Do not fire the machine guns until the enemy is within range and you have him squarely within your sights.
4. Attack when the enemy least expects it or when he is preoccupied with other duties such as observation, photography or bombing.
5. Never turn your back and try to run away from an enemy fighter. If you are surprised by an attack on your tail, turn and face the enemy with your guns.
6. Keep your eye on the enemy and do not let him deceive you with tricks. If your opponent appears damaged, follow him down until he crashes to be sure he is not faking.
7. Foolish acts of bravery only bring death. The Jasta must fight as a unit with close teamwork between all pilots. The signal of its leaders must be obeyed

Some of the things are a bit irrelevant to the game, but a lot of it is true. [/code]
