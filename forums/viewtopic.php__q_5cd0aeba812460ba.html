** UPDATED: 22-MAY-2007: Fixed some outdated information, added supported platforms, and made some other general nips and tucks.


* SMALL ROCKETS *


Q: Weren't you originally called Fiendish Entertainment?

A: No.

Fiendish Entertainment was a subsidiary of Criterion Software Ltd., (originally owned by Canon Research, but now owned by EA.) This is the same Criterion Software that makes the "Burnout" racing games for consoles today.

Criterion's management -- for reasons too tiresome to go into -- decided to close down Fiendish towards the end of 2000. This left Jonathan, who was Fiendish's manager at the time, with two options: forget Fiendish ever happened, or set up his own company instead. Jon chose the latter and Small Rockets is the result. Most of Fiendish's employees came with him, although they have all moved on to other companies now.

When Small Rockets was set up, they bought a number of games from Fiendish Entertainment's remains. Unfortunately, they were unable to buy the customer database for legal reasons. For a few years, ex-Fiendish customers were able to explicitly transfer their details to SR, but this is now no longer possible.

* WINDOWS VISTA SUPPORT *

Q: Do you support Vista yet?

A: No, we do NOT officially support Windows Vista at this time. While most of our games do seem to work, there are some known issues which we will need to work around before we'll be comfortable adding "Vista" to our list of supported platforms.

Another factor is that Vista support from some major hardware manufacturers is still not 100%. Creative Labs won't have final drivers for their popular "Audigy 2/4" products until late 2007. Similarly, NVidia are both known to have issues with some of their products, although AMD/ATI seem to be okay.

At present, most of our games do appear to work on Vista, but only in Compatibility Mode and with the fancy Vista "Aero" GUI to be disabled, (or at least have with the translucency and other silly effects turned off).

* THE "RED ACE" GAMES *

I've been answering quite a few queries about which of our "Red Ace" titles is a sequel to which other title, and which game you need to own to get the free update to RAS Pro.

To clear up the confusion, here's a quick run-down...


"Hunt For The Red Baron". (Early 2000. Released by Fiendish Entertainment.)
- Available to buy: NO.
- Supported: NO.
- Platforms: Microsoft Windows 95/98/Me

This was actually the first, and oldest, of what became the "Red Ace" series of games. This game was originally designed and developed by "Fiendish Entertainment" (RIP).

It was later discovered that "The Red Baron" was not only the name of another, superficially similar, game, hence the name-change to...


"Master Of The Skies: The Red Ace". (Late 2000.)
- Available to buy: YES.
- Supported: YES.
- Platforms: Windows 95/98/Me/XP/Vista (only in Compatibility Mode).

This is the name by which the game is still known. It is still available from our website and is primarily a single-player game. Aside from some minor tweaks to the code, this is basically the same game as "...Red Baron", but with a different title screen.


"Red Ace Squadron" (2001.)
- Available to buy: NO. (Replaced by Red Ace Squadron Pro.)
- Supported: YES.
- Platforms: Windows 95/98/Me/XP/Vista (only in Compatibility Mode).

NOTE: This is the game you must have bought DIRECTLY through our own website in order to qualify for the update to RAS Pro.

This game was the official sequel to "Master Of The Skies: The Red Ace". It had completely new levels, new missions and improved multiplayer support.

At the time, broadband was still very rare in the UK, hence the focus on LAN-based and modem-based multiplayer support in this release. This was finally remedied last year with the release of...


"Red Ace Squadron Pro" (2005.)
- Available to buy: YES.
- Supported: YES.
- Platforms: Windows 95/98/Me/XP/Vista (only in Compatibility Mode).

This is an update -- not a sequel! -- to "Red Ace Squadron". It removes the now-useless modem features and vastly improves the multiplayer gaming experience over the internet and local network. The server has been revamped. It does NOT contain new content: the missions, maps and other content remain otherwise untouched. Even the title screen still says "Red Ace Squadron".

This version replaced the previous release and, if you bought the original "Red Ace Squadron" directly from us, your license code will also work on this updated version.

Please do not ask us for a free upgrade if you bought through another supplier as we are ONLY offering RAS Pro to our own customers. (We see only a fraction of the money you pay to other resellers. From CD versions, we're lucky if we get a dollar from each sale.)


*

That's the story so far.

The future holds at least this much:

* There is a full-on sequel, called "Red Ace Squadron 2", in the works. This will be a completely new game, with a new graphics engine, new maps, new missions, new planes, etc.

* There is a plan to "remaster" many of our older games using the new engine developed for "Red Ace Squadron 2". These will have the same levels and graphics, but will also boast many of the new features supported by the new engine, including better joystick/joypad support, improved networking and other neat stuff.


Release dates aren't confirmed. "As soon as they're ready" is the best I can offer right now.
