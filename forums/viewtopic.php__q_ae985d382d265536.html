Windows Vista Complaints

by Geometrian on Mon Jul 16, 2007 10:35 am

Premium. The issues I have with Vista are small, but there are a lot of them. For example, among other complaints:

-You can't copy the path directories in a window.
-Vista assumes you're not an admin. even if you have the only user account.
-Vista's ctrl-alt-del command exits to the login screen, and offers you several options.
-There is no built-in antivirus software, even though Windows is the biggest hacker target in the world. (Note: preinstalled trial version of Norton Antivirus was included without uninstaller (had to download it). It still runs a process of Norton's even though it's uninstalled).
-Everything is incompatible.
-Sound recorder only saves in .wma format which is useless.
-Online updates must use Internet Explorer.
-Vista is more expensive than mac software.
-Microsoft (and its partners) cram a new computer's hard drive with trial software that is useless unless you buy the whole thing. My laptop's hard drive, new, had 6 GIGABYTES of the stuff.
-Transparency effects are cool, but cause some programs to have problems, like RAS.
-Drivers are a constant source of problems.
-Blown up icons on desktop look (understandably) pixelated.
-Firewall blocks itself!
-Command Line application is close to impossible to find.
-Start menu feels cramped; not opening into desktop.
-"Gadgets" are plagiarized from Mac's "Widgets".
-Windows directory is immense (around 8.3 GB) and contains data from previous versions of Windows.
-Previous versions of help files as new as Dec. 2006 are not supported and help from programs older than that is unavailable.
-Control Panel icons have been renamed, and some features such as "Display" have been filed in other things (in this case "Personalization").
-Microsoft Word 2006 is preinstalled, incompatible and has no uninstaller.
-Media center feels clumsy, inaccessible, and in general worse than any other free program such as iTunes.
-Versions are not kept track of, causing download and installations of updates for Vista Ultimate and graphics cards I don't have.
-User error warnings (Like "You must be ___ user" or "Must be admin.") are substituted for errors like "You cannot delete files in use" or "This program is a virus!" or "Warning! The system has encountered a fatal error and must die. Save work in 3, 2, 1...".
-Error messages that do not do the above are cryptic and have strange numbers ("Error number ______") that are undocumented.
-Then there's just weird: "There has been an error in creating this window." (So everything's OK then?)(Like mac on that one: "The application 'errorraise.[something]' has unexpectedly quit.")
-Start menu circular thing is plagiarized from Linux.
-The help window that pops up over it saying "start" has always been weird to me. (You press "start" to shut down?).
-The default, however, for the power button is "sleep". To change the power button to make it shut down requires slogging through various menus to find a hidden control panel.

...and there's not even a feedback site on windows.com to make suggestions!

Incidentally, I don't blame you, I blame Microsoft. Like XP, they have a way to go to make their product work.

by stimarco on Thu Jul 26, 2007 4:47 am

Dash234 wrote:is the new game and all the other games going to have macintosh compatibility? and are there any new game updates


The plan is to make the new game engine cross-platform, so yes, Mac support is on the cards.

New updates on RAS 2 aren't available yet. Jon got married recently, so there's a bit of personal stuff going on again, but most of the invisible behind-the-scenes code is coming together. (His most recent work has been on GUI handling and font rendering, which were both poorly handled in the old engine and don't work well at all in Vista.)

On the subject of Vista: support *is* planned for it. It's just that we don't make a habit of supporting a v1.0 release of *any* version of Windows. (Windows XP wasn't officially supported until well after SP1.) It takes a while for all the niggly bugs and quirks to get ironed out of any major application and Windows is just a really, really big, complex app.

In fairness to Vista, there are some bits MS have done right, but it just doesn't feel like 5 years and umpteen billion dollars' worth of improvements over XP. It sure as hell isn't worth the ridiculous sums of money MS are charging for it here in the UK -- think US prices and multiply them by two. MS -- and a few other corporations, like Adobe -- really do need to learn a lesson about currency exchange. I, for one, won't be buying it until MS reduce the price here.
