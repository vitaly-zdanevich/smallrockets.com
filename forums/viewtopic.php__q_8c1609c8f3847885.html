Just to make this clear: WE DO NOT SUPPORT VISTA YET. We don't even mention Vista in our recommended / minimum system requirements. There are forum FAQs discussing this. It's not like we've made any secret of it. Hell, we didn't even support XP until after SP 1 was out.

Most of our games were designed for the Win95/98 generation of Windows, so they need a lot of work to fix up. Part of Jon's Grand Plan is to port some of the worst-affected games into the new engine he's building for RAS 2. This means RAS 2's engine has to be powerful enough to handle *any* kind of game, not just RAS 2 itself.

Vista is a poor excuse of an update, with a number of major interface and architectural flaws. Hardware support is still patchy -- many users of Creative Labs' hardware are going to be in for a shock. And, given Vista's pricing in the EU -- and the UK in particular -- it'll be a cold day in Hell before I splash out that kind of cash for something that is, basically, "Windows XP: Mutton-Dressed-As-Lamb Edition".

Sorry about the rant, but the way some folks here bang on, you'd think Jon had a budget of millions to play with. Sorry, but that's just not the case.

It's coming. It'll be out when it's good and ready and not one moment before. Releasing a half-baked game does nobody any good.

[RAS]xray wrote:

As you say...Users will just click ok on UAC....so that would make THEM dumb...not the OS wouldn't it...


Oh HELL no. This is something I feel bloody strongly about after watching people struggling with lousy interface design for decades. It is high time people stopped blaming themselves or users for fundamental flaws in product and interface design. There is NO excuse for bad interface design in this day and age. None. Period. End of story. There are umpteen books on interface and interaction design now, from Donald A. Norman's seminal "Design of Everyday Things" through Jef Raskin's "The Humane Interface" and beyond to the likes of "About Face 2.0".

Granted, Apple aren't perfect either, but Microsoft are clearly guilty of putting eye-candy over functionality here. The Windows Vista UAC is dangerous because it comes up so often that users are pretty much *guaranteed* to learn to just dismiss it whenever it pops up. That it requires only a click, which rapidly becomes a subconscious mouse gesture, makes it utterly worthless and inherently useless as a security "feature". 99% of the times it appears, it'll be merely irritating and dismissing will become automatic. Thus, when it finally does ask about something genuinely dangerous, the user will probably not even be consciously aware of clicking the "OK" button, with predictable results.

Don't get me wrong: there are many things Vista has done right and it does indeed improve on XP in many areas, but it needs to be more than "XP+ Service Packs" to justify the price tag it has here in the EU.
