Re: Cool ideas for RAS2

by stimarco on Thu Mar 17, 2011 3:13 am

No, it's not too late, but there are limits to what Jon can add given the timeframe.

A cockpit view may be possible; the planes are simple enough that an artist wouldn't need to add that much detail to create a decent enough view.

The rear gunnery position is a tricky one. The "Red Ace" games aren't meant to be realistic flight simulators, but arcade games. Their emphasis is on fun, not accuracy, so if a historical fact proves inconvenient, it'll be ignored!

The problem is, the Red Ace games are already historically inaccurate as the synchronised guns we're familiar with from the movies were actually a late WW1 invention; for much of the war, not only were dogfights very rare, but the pilot simply pointed an ordinary at the other guy and took pot-shots at the enemy plane while flying his kite with his free hand. Furthermore, many planes of that era were two-seaters designed for observing enemy lines and, during the "pushes", communicating their intel with their (hopefully advancing) comrades on the ground. The chap in the observer's seat often had his own gun too. Our games don't go into all this. (Surprisingly, the rockets and bombs are based, albeit loosely, on historical events!)

Implementing a player-controlled rear gunner would add complexity to a game that's intended to be easy to pick up and play, so, wearing my game designer's hat, I don't think I'd include it. Here's my reasoning:

Unlike the real thing, there's only one real person manning each plane. If we included a rear-gunner viewpoint, how would you fly the plane? We'd have to reverse the mouse controls temporarily, which would confuse many gamers. And, of course, you wouldn't be able to see where you're going while this was happening. In reality, the pilot would always have that front view—and a much wider field of vision than a typical screen or TV can physically display—so adding a rear-gunner view would make the game harder than the real thing: that rear gunner didn't have to worry about flying too.

We could add a cooperative multiplayer option for such planes, but the "rear gunner" player would effectively be playing a simple "on-rails" shooter.

All that said, an automated rear gunner or observer may be an option, though this would require careful balancing. You don't want a useless rear gunner who can't hit a barn door from thirty inches, but neither do you want a gunner who's too accurate. The term "Artificial Intelligence", especially when applied to games, is often wildly inaccurate. A correctly programmed AI would never, ever, miss, so maintaining the illusion of human-like reactions and skills is achieved by deliberately handicapping the computer, forcing it to make mistakes, otherwise the players the computer fights won't stand a chance.

So I won't rule out the computer-controlled option. But the player-controlled option is unlikely to appear in RAS 2, unless there's strong demand for it.
