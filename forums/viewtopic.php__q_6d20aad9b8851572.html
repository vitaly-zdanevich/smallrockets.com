Hmm wrote:So can we just get one question answered ? Is SmallRockets still going or not ? There doesn't look like much has changed in years. Also isn't a one man engine always going to look pretty ropey compared to engines made with far larger teams ? Especially if that engine is diluted into a "platform" as it were ?


In order:

1. Yes, Small Rockets is still going. It's a hobby, not a full-time job. Jon wants to change it back into his full-time job.

2. Nothing much has changed in years? Aside from updating the forum software; releasing some old games as free; updating a bunch of installers to make them more Vista-friendly (though this hasn't proven enough); writing a ton of new code, and firefighting some major server problems... no. Not much.

3. A one-man engine will be as good as that one man. Since the one man in question is Jonathan Small, who builds these things for *fun*, and whose previous game engine for Criterion's "SubCulture" was used as the starting point for what became one of the most popular middleware engines ever -- it was used in "Grand Theft Auto III", some of Criterion's own "Burnout" series and even by the likes of Konami -- then I think it's fair to say it's extremely unlikely to be complete and utter crap.

Another point to remember is that Jon isn't planning to sell his engine to anyone else, so he can make the back-end tools as esoteric and cheap as he likes, so long as they get the job done.
