They don't make 'em like they used to. If you're one of those people who thinks that games haven't really got better since the days of twitch game-play, tons of explosions and one-hit-and-you're-dead, you're in for a treat.

The latest title from Small Rockets, purveyors of big games in small packages, is an unashamed old-skool arcade game. Iron Monkey, a name which refers to the eponymous fighter craft piloted by the player, harks back to the golden era of vertically-scrolling shoot-em-ups.

The plot, for what it's worth, goes as follows. After centuries of false alarms, it's finally happened: the aliens have landed, and they're not here to watch re-runs of Ricki Lake.

The game begins after the invaders have already given their best shot at wiping out mankind. Piloting the advanced prototype craft Iron Monkey, you must lead the fight-back: fly through the ruins of what's left on Earth, up into space and inside the alien's mothership. And then hitch a lift back to the aliens' home planet to destroy that for good measure too.

"We've tried to capture the essence of what made those old games so addictive, and add state-of-the-art 3D visuals and effects," says lead programmer James Brown, previously responsible for divine puzzler Tower Of The Ancients.

The Iron Monkey team, comprising James, Paul Boulden (artist) and Adrian Moore (designer), has created a visually stunning environment. Although the game-play remains true to its 2D roots, all objects are rendered in 3D, lending the graphics a powerful sense of depth and solidity. The lighting effects are particularly dramatic, with atmospheric shadows generated in real time from multiple light sources. The environment can even be blasted and deformed by the player's fire!

The aim of the game is simple. You must destroy as many of the enemy craft and ground units as possible, while avoiding the storm of enemy bullets that will inevitably be launched in your direction. The enemies are devious, attacking relentlessly and circling round behind your craft to catch you offguard.

Destroying certain craft gives the player access to power-ups, including speed, shields and extra weapons such as miniguns, homing missiles and mines. The add-ons are cumulative, so as more power-ups are collected, their potency increases - later versions actively defend your ship from enemy fire, and add massive secondary weapons with extravagant firepower.

"Iron Monkey has the same addictive quality as classic shoot-em-ups such as Slapfight, Twin Cobra and R-Type," says designer Adrian Moore. "Sometimes the screen's so full of bullets that you'll think you'll never get through, but the one-more-go factor will keep you coming back until you beat that final boss."

Iron Monkey is scheduled for release in April, and will be available for download from the Small Rockets website.
