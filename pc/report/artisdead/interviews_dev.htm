Interviews - Adrian Moore


To get some in depth information for audience, we interviewed Adrian Moore, Art Is Dead's designer to find out more about this new game...

’Art is Dead’ is the latest release from Small Rockets Games, tell us what the game is about?

You have inherited a stately home from your recently-deceased Uncle Arthur Morgan-Ffyre. He was a keen art collector and accumulated many highly valuable works of art during his lifetime. The catch is, he doesn’t believe you truly appreciate art, so he has hidden all the paintings from you (except one), and devised a series of challenges for you, from beyond the grave! The challenges are set within the works of art themselves, and you must achieve a certain number of "appreciation points" in each painting in order for you to progress and reclaim all the paintings in the house. The challenges take the form of simple shooting games, which you can play with a lightgun or a mouse.

It has an interesting theme, what inspired the original idea?

I wanted to do a lightgun game because I had really enjoyed Point Blank in my local bowling alley, and thought it would be a challenge to devise a similarly light-hearted pick-up-and-play game, but with a new twist.

Small Rockets games’ head, Jon Small, had the idea of paintings "coming to life", which I thought we should run with, and the rest of the story came together through team brainstorming sessions and also input from James Leach, a writer we hired to do the scripting.

What sort of levels can people expect to see?

They are very diverse. You’ll see Mona Lisa with a "flip top head" opening up to reveal flying hippos and elephants, a volcano spewing out molten boulders, a traditional clay pigeon shooting level, a peaceful village scene under threat from alien spacecraft, amongst many, many more!

In what ways does it differ from other games in its genre?

There isn’t really anything else like this out there, that I’m aware of. Most lightgun games seem to be taking themselves quite seriously – following a linear path through an environment, shooting dinosaurs or zombies. I guess the closest game to Art is Dead is Point Blank, but then you can’t download Point Blank from the internet and play it on your PC!

’Art Is Dead’ is very atmospheric yet has a very wicked sense of humor, especially using the famous paintings?

That’s true. I, like the rest of the team, really hope that people will sit up and take notice of this game. It definitely seems to provoke a reaction. I hope that people will see some of the works of art we used in the game, in a new light, having played Art is Dead! I also hope it will make people laugh.

What is ‘Party Mode’?

Party mode allows any number of people to compete in an Art is Dead competition. You set the number of challenge wins required to win the competition, and then take it in turns to try to become the highest scorer in the chosen challenges. We’re very keen to for Art is Dead to be played as much in multiplayer mode, as in singleplayer mode.

The game utilizes a lightgun (as well as the mouse), how does this enhance the game’s enjoyment?

Both methods of play are fun, but people will favour one or the other. The lightgun offers a more tactile game and will appeal to people who like guns, and the mouse offers a more direct way into the game anyone will be able to play Art is Dead because we allow it to be played with the mouse.

Can more than one person play the game with light guns?

Yes. In the 2 player mode it is possible to compete in the same challenge simultaneously, each player using their own gun.

Does ‘Art Is Dead’ continue our line of accessible games?

Absolutely. There are no complex rules to learn. Simply pick up the mouse or lightgun, aim and shoot!

When can we expect a downloadable beta version of ‘Art Is Dead’?

Expect to see it sometime this year.

Thanks Adrian!
