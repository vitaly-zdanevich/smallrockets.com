Interviews - Jon Askew

As well as graphics and good game design, 'Art Is Dead' features interesting technology ultilizing the lightgun, programmer Jon Askew answered questions about this aspect of the game's development...

How difficult was it to code ‘Art Is Dead’?

The code in general is not too complicated, however there were a few tricky problems to figure out.

Was there any problems where the games utilizes the lightgun technology?

There were some problems with calibration but they are working fine now.

Any tips on playing the game?

All the levels are predetermined by the designer, including the movement of all the objects. One of the ways you can improve at playing the game is to learn the patterns in each stage, giving you a better chance of getting that elusive "Perfect!!".

Was the game great fun to make?

Very much so, from getting the first objects moving about on the screen, the first time we could use the lightguns, right through to seeing what Ade has put into the new levels as they are scripted. Always been something new, and fun .

What’s your favorite level in ‘Art Is Dead’?

Tough one. Artistically I really like the duck shoot scene, the landscape in that is very relaxing. But that is only half the question, and gameplay wise I’m torn between the soup cans (got to love the simplicity) and the volcano level.

Thanks Jon!
