Interviews - Robert Heald

Small Rockets approaches game production in a unique way, one that benefits the public as well as the designers. Robert Heald, who is Art Is Dead's Producer, tells us more ...

How complete is ‘Art Is Dead’?

The game is currently 75% complete and on schedule.

Have you worked closely with the artists and developers to define the look and atmosphere of ‘Art Is Dead’?

The look of ‘Art Is Dead’ was largely determined by Adrian, Anthony and Tomas [game designer and artists]; my role is to ensure that everything works together smoothly and the game comes in on time, but we develop our games collectively so I’ve had my input. Even with a very small team you have to make sure there’s a common vision of how the game should be.

What is your favorite part of the game?

I think it has to be the humour in many of the levels my particular favourite is the one based around the painting of Whistler’s Mother, where you have to catch the flies before the old lady shoots out her tongue and eats them. Humour’s very rare in games at the moment there could be much more of it all round.

Did you design/develop any of the levels/elements yourself ? If so which ones?

Apart from input into the overall design of the game, I’m responsible for the scripting (object movement) on some levels, including Da Vinci’s spinning man, the Monet bridge level and the Cezanne Fruitbowl.

How difficult is it to prioritize the elements of a game, for example game play versus artistic requirements?

Game play will always come first there’s no point producing something that looks great but is no fun to play. Having said that, it’s often the little touches that make a game special. For example, if you hang around in the mansion rooms in ‘Art Is Dead’ for a while, your late uncle will appear and make comments about the rooms and what he did when he was alive; it’s got nothing to do with the gameplay, but it adds immensely to the feeling that the mansion is a real place once owned by a real person.

Thanks Robert!
