Interviews - Anthony Callaghan

One of the most impressive features of Art Is Dead is the level of detail in the graphics. Like all our games titles, the artists have spent a great deal of time developing the look and feel of the game. We asked Anthony Callaghan one of the game's artists (along with Tomas O'Sullivan) to tell us more...

Clearly art plays a major part in this new game, it must have been great fun giving famous paintings the ‘Small Rockets’ treatment?

Yes art is the name of the game in more ways than one. It’s very satisfying to take works of art which are seen as serious pieces and creating fun ways to make them come alive.

Does ‘Art Is Dead’ have a very high level of graphical detail, considering its ‘artistic’ theme?

Art is dead showcases many different styles of art past and present. Some of the art are our own masterpieces ( well we would like to think so ), which makes for a variety in levels of detail in the game. For instance the modern art levels have a much simpler form compared to the works of the old masters.

How long is the design stage of a game the size of ‘Art Is Dead’?

With Small Rockets games having such a short turnaround, the design of the game is a process which carries on throughout it’s production. The main bulk of course at the beginning when the structure and basics of gameplay are laid down.

It is left to us artists to create the style of the game but input is welcome from any member of the team. We are free to change any elements of the design as the game progresses to bring it all together into one coherent style.

The Mansion interiors are very impressive, how were they created?

All the mansion interiors in the game were created in 3D Studio Max taking reference from many period styles.

Do you have to have a 3-D graphics card installed to use the game?

Yes a 3-d graphics card is compulsory.

What previous games have the ‘Art Is Dead’ artists worked on?

Our last endeavor was "Master of the Skies: The Red Ace"

What is your favorite level in the game?

That’s a difficult one. There are many levels that I like in the game. But I think that Raphael's "The sistine madonna" is my favourite. It was good fun changing the cherubs into little devil cherubs and making their little wings flap. It's like doing one of those Monty Python cartoons.

Artistically what were the main influences on the look of ‘Art Is Dead’?

Well the story is of your deceased uncle Arthur who leaves you all these paintings. But he’s still hanging around as a ghost haunting the mansion. So the look of the game had to have that dark ghostly atmosphere. I think Tim Burton was an influence in the look of a lot of the menu backdrops. But when it comes to the levels, well the paintings speak for themselves.

What did you enjoy most about working on ‘Art Is Dead’?

Well fooling about with famous paintings of course. I think for me the best part was the brainstorming meetings. We came up with so many wacky ideas for the challenges in the game, just looking at a painting and seeing how we could basically make it fun to play and fun to look at.

Thanks Anthony!

 Adrian Moore, Game Designer
